How the split works

  • Zigzag seams (default): each tile ends along the lattice itself. Bars on a seam print at half width, so two neighboring tiles nest together and their half bars meet as one normal bar. The seam disappears into the pattern. The outer border is cut straight at different points, so border joints and lattice joints don't line up, which makes the frame stiffer.
  • Straight seams: tiles are cut on a straight grid and every cut edge gets a rail half the border width. Two glued tiles read as one frame division, like a shoji muntin. Splice plates glue across each seam on the back.
  • Half-lap with locking teeth (default): seam bars are split by depth. Base tiles keep the front half, addition tiles the back half, and they alternate like a checkerboard. Teeth on the addition tiles drop into notches in the base tiles and alternate direction, so tiles can't slide apart. Addition tiles export flipped so their teeth print without supports. Set grid bar height to the depth you want: 12 mm gives 60 layers, split 30 and 30. With snap-fit teeth on, each tooth has a 0.25 mm barb that clicks under a lip in its notch, so tiles lock front to back too.
  • Bowtie keys: on the back of each seam, both tiles get half of a dovetail-shaped pocket. A printed bowtie key presses in and its flared ends hook both tiles, so they can't pull apart. The pocket stops 1 mm short of the front, so nothing shows. No glue.
  • Border pegs: the border's backing lattice carries pegs that drop into round holes on the back of the outer tiles, locking the border to the grid. Fit clearance sets how tight keys and pegs are; print one tile, a key and a border piece first and adjust by 0.05 mm.
  • Wall pegs (default): each tile hangs on the wall by itself. Two hidden sockets on its back push onto pegs on small screw-on wall pucks, like a floating shelf on rods. Pull any tile straight off to swap it; screw up two more pucks from the template to grow the design. Zigzag seams make neighbors nest; nothing is glued or clipped together.
  • Hidden cleats: the border is deeper than the lattice, so the panel stands off the wall like a floating shelf. Hook lips hidden behind the lattice hang from the top border and drop behind printed wall cleats. The export includes the cleats and a full-size drill template (print wall_template.svg at 100%).
  • Separate border: border pieces print on their own. Each carries a thin one-row lattice on the back that sits behind the outer row of the grid. Border STLs come flipped, so that lattice prints flat on the bed with no supports.
  • The pattern is computed once across the whole frame, so the lattice lines up across every seam.
  • Each STL is a set of overlapping bars. Bambu Studio, PrusaSlicer, Orca and Cura merge them on slicing. If a slicer complains, run its repair or "merge parts" step.
  • Print flat, face down, with a brim. Large thin lattices lift at the corners: PETG or PLA on a textured PEI sheet behaves best.
