How it works

Three steps, no glue, nothing showing.

1

The frame goes up

Small standoffs screw to the wall and hide behind the frame bar. The frame clicks onto them. Every screw head sits out of sight.

2

Tiles press in from the front

Each tile pushes onto two hidden wall pucks. The zigzag seams follow the lattice lines, so tiles nest together and the joins vanish into the pattern.

3

Change it anytime

Pull a tile, change its insert or backer color, press it back. Or upload a photo and the builder matches every cell to ten stocked filament colors.

Where two printed tiles meet: the zigzag seam follows the lattice geometry, so joins disappear completely into the pattern.

Specs

Built for the printer you already have.

Printers Any bed from 180 × 180 mm. Presets for Bambu P2S, X1C, P1S, A1 and A1 mini, Prusa MK4S, Core One and XL, Creality Ender-3 V3 and K1 Max.
Example panel 600 × 400 mm: 6 tiles and 10 frame pieces, under 800 g of filament.
Patterns Asanoha, kikkō, mitsukude, sankaku (triangle), kaku (square) and hishi (diamond). Cells 30, 40 or 60 mm.
Depth 6 mm lattice in a 7 mm frame. Sits 5 mm off the wall, or 20 mm with the LED backlight.
Material PLA or PETG, 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers. No supports on any part.
Assembly Snap-fit, no glue. A four-step test kit tunes the fit to your printer in 0.05 mm steps.
Hardware #6 screws and wall anchors. Nothing shows from the front.

Designed by

Travis Hergenreder

Denver, Colorado · October 2026

"The kumiko kits I found were finished the day you printed them. I wanted one with room to grow."

Living Lattice is an original design. The tiles, joints, frame and wall hardware were built from scratch so a panel can keep changing: swap a color, try a new insert, add a section later, all without taking the frame off the wall.

Asanoha, kikkō and the other patterns are centuries-old Japanese designs that belong to everyone. What's new is how the pieces fit together.
